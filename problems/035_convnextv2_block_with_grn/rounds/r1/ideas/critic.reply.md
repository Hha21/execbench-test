### Audit

The first portal result (0.3038, with per-workload Tb) disproves or weakens several r0 premises.

- **Tb is not eager.** Tb is 0.34–0.59 × the rented eager time. Eager on the portal's 1500 MHz clock would be slower still, so the baseline is a compiled PyTorch version 2–3.5× faster than eager. Only 1 of 16 workloads scores above 0.5 (1,96,56,56 at 0.511).
- **Tsol is negligible.** 125.9 GFLOP / 70 µs = 1.80 PF/s, so Tsol uses the tensor rate. Tsol is far below every Tb, and S ≈ Tb/(t+Tb). That makes relative cuts worth about the same in every band: predict_score gives 0.007–0.008 per 10% per band.
- **The anchor is clock-bound everywhere.** Portal/rented is 1.30–1.32 in all three bands, which is the clock ratio (1965/1500). So rented SIMT rates must be scaled by 0.76 before use.
- **The main framing is overstated: "GEMM rate is the problem, tensor cores move the score".** The leader needs only 16–42 TF/s on the S band. That is below the fp32 SIMT peak, and true on 6 of 15 rows. The anchor runs 1,192,28,28 at 4 TF/s, 7% of the SIMT peak. Its phase 1 alone takes 333 µs at 32,768,7,7 for 0.12 GFLOP of conv. Much of the loss is in structure (phase 1, weight transposes, barriers, split-K atomics), not the MMA rate.
- **The dwconv's importance is overstated.** Even a second full SIMT dwconv pass at the largest size costs about 0.002 of mean score. Recompute designs should not be ruled out on dwconv cost.
- **FP16 as "the" format is not justified.** The rate makes no difference to the ceiling (TF32 0.977 vs FP16 0.988). The leader needs at most 174 TF/s, 19% of the TF32 peak. Meanwhile FP16 adds a per-call weight conversion and in-kernel conversions; TF32 needs only round-to-nearest.
- **Two ledger statuses and one dead end rest on weak evidence.** H5 ("supported") is confounded. The cooperative-launch "dead end" is a single comparison at the one size where barriers cannot matter.
