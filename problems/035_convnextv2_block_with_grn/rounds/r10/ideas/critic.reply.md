### Audit

I couldn't read G's r9 card: `get_kernel` returned 91 KB, more than the tool limit, and I have no shell here to slice it. The claims below come from the ledger, the notebook stamps, first-principles arithmetic and one `predict_score` call.

1. **The TF32 zero-conversion idea, which r9 E1 recommends as the next lever, leaves out where the W1 read would happen.** Today all 148 CTAs read W1 in parallel during phase A, which costs about 2.5 µs. With direct TF32 reads, only the CTAs that own a GEMM1 tile would read it: 24 CTAs at 2,768 and 96 at 8,768.
   - Each tile needs 128 × 768 × 4 B = 393 KB of fp32 W1, against 197 KB of fp16 today.
   - At the measured per-SM ingest rates that is about 4 µs (from L2, 94 GB/s/SM) or about 10 µs (cold DRAM, 40 GB/s/SM).
   - kind::tf32 also needs twice as many MMA instructions, and r9 measured a per-instruction floor (point 2).
   - So the idea probably loses unless a microbenchmark says otherwise.
2. **The "1SM floor of about 105 clk per MMA, set by fetching the A slice" contradicts r6's pair probe.** In that probe, each SM of a pair did the same 128×128×16 work in 86 clk per instruction. So the floor is not fixed per-SM work. It may be an artefact of how the probe issues instructions in its loop.
   - Either way it muddies H31. At N = 128, 479 clk per stage is 4 MMAs at about 120 clk each, which is the floor itself, not an operand-ingest cap.
   - Tile-N choices, G's 224-token port plan and the TF32 question all depend on which explanation is right.
3. **The ledger is stale after E1.**
   - H39 is still "open", yet its premise is false: the plane copies were already issued at kernel entry.
   - H2/H18 and lineage D's list of remaining terms still say the conv runs 15× its FMA bound at 8,768. E1's stamps show the conv ends 0.9 µs after its copies land; the rest of the time is spent queued behind the W1 stream.
   - Barrier release is recorded as 1.5–2 µs; the measured maxima are 1.0–1.5 µs.
   - H33 is "open" although its refutation clause fired on 4 of 6 rows.
   - A next round could spend effort speeding up a C = 768 conv that isn't the problem.
4. **"The S rows are below the noise" overstates the case.**
   - `predict_score`: a 20% cut on the S band gives +0.0128, more than the +0.0076 needed to pass the live #1. A 10% cut is worth +0.0062 there against +0.0069 on M.
   - The S rows are latency chains. At 1,192 the weight pass takes 2.6–3.1 µs for 1.2 MB, about 0.2 µs of bandwidth. The stubbed single-launch floor is 4.6 µs against 18–19 µs rented.
   - Portal noise of ±1.2 µs hides 1 µs changes, not 4 µs ones.
5. **E1's flat counter barrier on tc_kernel went to the portal on thin evidence.** It is hand-written synchronisation in front of TMA reads of data that generic stores wrote, and it was checked only by run_tests (16 workloads × 10 rounds, each passing). The single-seed fp64 check and the STACK check were skipped. A rare race would zero a workload, costing about 0.045 of mean per row.
