### Audit

The most useful evidence is already in the archive but missing from the ledger. Submission #65570 (r1-lib-cublaslt-fp16-v3) failed with code 101 on 8 workloads, yet the 8 workloads that ran have portal latencies. Their portal/rented ratio is **1.27–1.28 on every row**, including both S rows and the 616 MB row. That ratio is close to the 1965/1500 clock ratio, which means three things:

- The ledger note "expect ~1.05 for DRAM-bound parts" does not hold for this problem.
- The explanation that each launch costs 6–17 µs because of "dependent global round trips" is doubtful. Latency measured in nanoseconds, or gaps waiting on the CPU, would not stretch by exactly the clock ratio at S. The S-row time looks like SM cycles.
- The emulator's projections of about 0.59 for lineage A are roughly right. The ±0.008 is still too tight, because the same v3 code measured 6 µs slower per call in the v3fix session. The lab notebook also copies v3's numbers under v3fix, so it records a result that was never measured.

Three ceilings that steer the lineages are stale or contradicted by r1 data:

- Lineage A assumes about 2 µs per launch. Its own probe refuted that.
- H11 says the fixed floor F sets the score. Lineage B measured F at 4.6 µs, yet its S rows take 28–55 µs. The phase chains set the score, not F.
- H4 says a cooperative launch costs about the same as three launches. B measured the cooperative launch 6.6 µs (18%) faster at 1,192,28,28, and 4.6 µs vs 9.9 µs with the phases stubbed.

The r1 audit's "FP16's 2× rate buys no score, use TF32" is wrong. It rests on the leader's average FLOP rate, but measured cuBLASLt TF32 is 1.4–2.1× slower even at the smallest shapes, where the FLOP rate is irrelevant.

H2 and the card make the dwconv look like a minor term because they price it at its FMA bound (134 µs). The measured conv is 446–954 µs rented at the largest size and 234 µs at 32,128,64,64, which is 25–53% of total time. Bringing it near its bound is worth about +0.015 of mean score across the top two sizes. That is more than any S-row tweak.

The precision margin is stated as about 5σ with "0.000% beyond tolerance", but that is an rms argument. Lineage B logged max errors of 1.9–2.8e-3 against atol 2.4e-3. At σ ≈ 4.5e-4, a 77M-element workload with atol 2.4e-3 would put about 10 elements past tolerance per round. That workload would then pass only because of the 98% matched ratio, which is on the forbidden list. Lineage A never logged its max error.
